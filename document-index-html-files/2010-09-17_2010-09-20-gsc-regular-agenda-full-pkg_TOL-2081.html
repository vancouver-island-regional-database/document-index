Adopts the LEED Canada Building Rating System as the rating system by which to assess building performance; i 2. Establishes the LEED Silver level of building performance as the minimum acceptable building standard for all newly constructed civic buildings greater than 465 sq. m. (5.000 sq, ft) and incorporates sustainable building practices into all projects that are less than this threshold; 3. Incorporates sustainable building practices into all renovation and/or upgrade projects for ail facilities that are developed, owned or managed | by the Town. i PURPOSE: The purpose of this report is to recommend to Council the adoption of a Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design (LEED) construction rating system as a means to encourage sustainable high performance construction practices for Town owned buildings and building construction projects. This policy would require that all civic buildings that are owned, constructed, financed or utilized by the Town of Ladysmith be constructed to performance standards comparable to that of LEED standards and all buildings that are under this threshold (including renovations and/or retrofits to existing buildings) incorporate sustainable building practices. INTRODUCTION/BACKGROUND: In 2008, Council and the community participated in the Community Sustainability . Visioning Initiative. Council endorsed the award-winning final report which provides overall direction for taking action on sustainable development. In addition, energy reduction targets, initiatives and actions have been established for the community | under the guidance of the Environment Commission as part of the Community Energy Plan (Energy Plan) adopted by Council in 2008. Given the Town of Ladysmith’s ongoing commitment to the reduction of green house gas emissions (GHGs), and continuing support of the principles of sustainability, it is important that the Town reinforce this.commitment through the adoption of a leadership position that sety an example in the encouragement of the | ) | | I . | use of high performance building standards in the management and construction of its building infrastructure. The Energy Plan outlines that approximately 20% of Ladysmith’s GHG emissions came from buildings. Furthermore, approximately 51% (209 tonnes of GHGs) of total municipal (corporate) emissions come from buildings. With the intent of becoming carbon neutral by 2012 (as per the Climate Action Charter) it will be important to . improve the energy and efficiency performance of existing as well as new buildings. LEED Building Rating Systems: The LEED building rating system was developed by the US Green Building Council to provide building performance rating levels. This rating system was adapted by the : Canadian Green Building Council in 2002 to reflect Canadian environmental conditions. Under the LEED rating system, a project or building earns a series of credits for various building attrioutes that provide for of sustainable building performance (see Appendix 1). The LEED design process results in an environmentally sustainable building that provides significant long term benefits in reduced operational costs, as well as a reduced environmental impact and longer capital life expectancy. Current studies indicate that a typical additional capital investment of two percent in construction costs to incorporate LEED features will yield overall lifecycle cost savings of approximately ten times the initial capital investment, through the reduction of energy expenditures, and operational maintenance savings, as well as potential employee productivity improvements. For example, an initial upfront investment of up to $100,000 to incorporate green building features into a $5 million project would result in a savings of $1 million in today’s dollars over the life of the building. Environmentally sustainable building practices are being more and more implemented by the construction industry and as a result the incremental costs of ‘building green’ have come down substantially to the point where the level of LEED Silver has in effect become the building norm for sustainable construction practices. SCOPE OF WORK: It is recommended that LEED Silver be adopted as recommended in this staff report. ALTERNATIVES: Council could choose not to adopt a formal policy (such as the use of an environmental rating system like LEED) and instead strive to incorporate sustainable | building principles into civic projects. FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS: The cost implications of this recommendation will vary on a project by project basis. However the adoption of a LEED Silver construction standard on civic projects can be justified in terms of financial payback, given the operational cost savings and other benefits achieved. 5 | | The recommendation contained in this report discusses only the aspect of complying with the LEED certification. The report has been accepted by Council as a vision from our community.
