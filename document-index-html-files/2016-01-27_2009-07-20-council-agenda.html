Re: FACADE IMPROVEMENT DEVELOPMENT PERMIT APPLICATION - 41.1 FIRST
AVENUE AND 30 ROBERTS STREET (FUTUREWOKS CONSULTING INC.)

RECOMMENDATION(S):

1 As Council has implemented policies and guidelines that the Downtown is to
develop consistent with its heritage character and theme and applications DP
08-08 and DP 08-09 are a departure from the heritage colour scheme, that
Council request staff to work with the applicant for DP 08-08 and DP 08-09 to
revise the exterior colours to meet the Downtown Design Guidelines (heritage
palette).

2. That Council request staff to include a heritage colour consultant in the review
of the Downtown Design Guidelines, when this project is undertaken.

PURPOSE:
The purpose of this report is to present to Council two development permit proposals
for facade improvements in the downtown area.

INTRODUCTION/BACKGROUND:

The building owners have applied to undertake a fagade improvement to two
buildings located within the downtown area. The buildings are located at 414 First
Avenue and 30 Roberts Street.

While the issuance of fagade improvement development permits has been delegated
to the Manager of Development Services, this application is being presented to
Council as the proposed paint colours do not meet the design guidelines (heritage
palette) for the development permit area (colour statement below). These guidelines
originated from the Ladysmith Design Guidelines (1986) which emphasized that the
choice of colour is critical.

The overall colour scheme should be harmonious, and unify the various
elements of the facade. In general, earth tones and natural pigment colours
are the most appropriate choice. Colours that are unacceptable include
yellows, oranges, reds, blues, greens, fluorescent colours and plain white.

While the Town has not developed its own heritage palette, it has been the practice
to use historical collections and heritage colours from paint companies, such as

1


Benjamin Moore, Beauti-Tone, General Paint, ICI Paint and Cloverdale. Some paint
companies provide colour by building construction period.

Official Community Plan policy supports the continued planning and management of
the downtown in a manner consistent with its heritage character.

_ SCOPE OF WORK:
The proposal is to paint the exterior of two buildings using four colours and to replace
the canopy with a style similar to the Ladysmith Trading Co. canopy. The design
concept would create the look of three separate buildings facing First Avenue. The
canopy colour needs to be confirmed.

The requested colours are:
« Benjamin Moore-Buckhorn CC-510 which is from the Benjamin Moore
heritage palette.
« Benjamin Moore: Golden Nugget 2019-20; Venezuelan Sea 2054-30; and
Flame 2012-20 which are not from a heritage palette.

Heritage colours were suggested to the applicant by staff prior to consideration by
the HRAC. The applicant’s rationale for the proposed colours is to anchor the corner
building; attract attention to the east side of First Avenue; and provide vibrancy and
energy. In addition, the applicant advised that the Buckhorn colour will be at the
base of the building to allow for a calm pedestrian experience at street level.
Additional considerations presented by the applicant are that the existing colours of
buildings in town are too dull, faded and blend with one another (with the exception
of a few buildings on Roberts Street), and it is hoped that the proposed colours will
start a new trend in downtown Ladysmith.

While the proposed colour scheme is bold and vibrant, it does not reflect the design
guidelines (heritage palette). The heritage palette includes a range of colour choices.
Work done by the Vancouver Heritage Foundation in 2001 provides the following
insight into the use of authentic heritage colour and placement which is the direction
given by the design guidelines for facade improvements.

“Colour themes that are not authentic, such as the ‘Painted Ladies’ approach
to some Victoria homes, can be playful presentation of a restored building.
Nonetheless, colour use on our heritage buildings was part of the original
architectural design and intent. Authentic colour can be the finishing step in
restoring the integral presentation of heritage architecture. The use of
appropriate historic colours can reveal and emphasize the building’s original
style and appearance.1”

The proposed colour scheme could be revised to include a colour scheme from a
heritage palette / historical collection. It is recommended that the applicant review
the options and provide a revised scheme. For illustration only, the following
comparison colours are presented. (The proposed colours and heritage palette
comparison is available in Council Chambers for review.)

Re: RESOLUTION PLACE

RECOMMENDATION(S):

That Council reduce the capital expenditure for the amphitheatre (engineering) project from
$50,000 to $37,000 and increase funding for the removal of trees from the green space around
Resolution Place from $0 to $13,000.

PURPOSE:

The purpose of the report is to inform Council of the liability created by some dead and dangerous
trees located within the green space around Resolution Place and to present options to consider
for the future of the green space.

INTRODUCTION/BACKGROUND:

In April of 2009, Council received a report regarding the unsightly condition of the green space at
Resolution Place. An arborist report identified a number of dead and dangerous trees that shouid
be removed for safety reasons and a number of trees that need to be monitored in the future to
safeguard the area residents.

A meeting on site with the residents resulted in the Town agreeing to remove the dangerous trees.
A number of options for the future of the green space were discussed and a survey was distributed
for residents to complete.

At a subsequent neighbourhood meeting with the residents which took place on Monday, July 13th
the Town agreed to remove the dead and dangerous trees within three weeks. The Town also
agreed to conduct another meeting to determine which options the Town will consider for the
remaining area.

SCOPE OF WORK:
There are a total of 57 dead or dangerous trees that are marked for removal. Because of the size
and location of the trees, they can only be removed in small sections. The contractor proposes to

fall the trees in sections and chip the debris created while leaving the trunks at a location for the
Town to pick up and remove.

ALTERNATIVES:

1. Continue to issue notices when infractions occur.
2. Remove all the dead trees and monitor annygally as per the arborist report.


3. Remove all trees and develop into open space which can be maintained.

4. Remove damaged trees, raise the canopy on sound trees and develop into a tot lot and
maintainable walking trails.

5. Create lots as per attached and sell lots to help pay for removal of trees and develop the green
space.

6. Sell portions of the green space to the residents directly in front of the green space. All
property owners in a specific area would have to agree.

FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS:


The cost to cut and remove the trees and debris is estimated to be $13,000. One of the options
brought to the residents of the area is to establish some lots (see attached drawing) and use the
revenue from the sale of the lots to assist with the cost of the clearing and future development of
the green space.

There are no funds allocated for this project in 2009. Completion of the project can be funded by
reducing funding of another project. It is recommended that the amphitheatre (engineering)
capital project be reduced to accommodate this project.

LEGAL IMPLICATIONS:


The green space is owned by the Town and as such the Town is liable should one or more of the
trees fall causing person injury or property damage.

CITIZEN/PUBLIC RELATIONS IMPLICATIONS:

The green space is used by the public for various purposes such as parking vehicles and storing
unwanted goods. Property owners have requested that the area be cleaned up around their
properties. They have also been given the option of buying the portion of the green space behind
their property.

INTERDEPARTMENTAL INVOLVEMENT/IMPLICATIONS:

The Public Works Department will arrange for the removal of the trees and the Parks, Recreation
and Culture Department will assist with the clean up and/or disposai of the green space.

ALIGNMENT WITH STRATEGIC PRIORITIES:

The project is not identified as a strategic priority and costs associated with the tree removals is
not currently in the budget.

SUMMARY:

Due to liability and safety issues associated with the dangerous trees located in the green space
behind Resolution Place, the removal of all the dead and dangerous trees marked by an arborist
should be undertaken as soon as possible. Once the trees are removed a further meeting of the
area residents will be held to identify the preferred option to address the future development of

the green space.

resulting decisions to adopt, deny or defer approval of the name “Salish Sea” will be based on
conformance with established geographical naming principles, including comments received through
this consultation process. . :

The proponent describes this entity as an estuarine inland sea that is distinct from the Pacific Ocean to the west of
Juan de Fuca Strait (Canada)/Strait of Juan de Fuca (USA) and the marine waters north of Strait of Georgia: salinity,
tidal patterns and climate create habitat conditions that support plant, animal & marine species unique or limited
to the inland sea area —an integral ecological unit that should have Its own distinct name. Further, the proponent
maintains that the name “Salish Sea” is becoming established in the region, and cites the Tribes and First Nations
who meet in the annual Coast Salish Gathering, the participants at the Georgia Basin Puget Sound Research
Conferences (co-hosted by Environment Canada and Puget Sound Action Committee) and assorted community
Groups throughout the region, as important populations that routinely use the name “Salish Sea”.

The proponent also identifies improved ecosystem management as a compelling reason to adopt the name “Salish
Sea,” however, it is important to note that adoption of this or any name does not imply protection, or obligate
agencies to manage the entity being named. The single outward evidence of adoption of this name might be the
addition of a label on maps, charts & atlases produced by provincial, state and federal governments.
