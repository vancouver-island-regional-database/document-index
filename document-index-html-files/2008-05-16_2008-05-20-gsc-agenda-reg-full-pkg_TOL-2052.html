| Community Design Awards program. Nominations are welcome for construction, beautification, or environmental projects that enhance the community. . The Sommunity Design Awards program was originally launched to recognize projects that make an exceptional contribution to the community and support the objectives of the Officiat Community Plan (OCP), a comprehensive document that guides development in the Municipality. Projects that incorporate environmental sensitivity, attractive landscape design, innovative architectural and/or community development initiatives are judged against the five goals expressed in the OCP which include: Preserving the Rural Setting, Working With The Environment; Encouraging Complete Communities; Building Strong Neighbourhoods; and Ensuring Responsive Planning and Decision Making. Nominations are considered in four different categories: Residential, Industrial, Commercial and Public Use. Fast projects receiving Community Design Awards include: + Averill Creek Winery for its contribution to agriculture and agri-tourism. + Somenos Marsh Wildlife Society and partners for the Drinkwater Dock and its role in promoting fish and wildlife habitat and conservation vatues. ¢ Catalyst Crofton Mill for the energy savings resulting from the sustainable energy management plan. 4 Suncraft Construction Ltd. for an attractive 14-unit townhouse on Alexander Street. ¢# Cowichan Green Communities and partners for the Kinsman Park Community Garden. 2006 ¢ Kevin Fraser of K2 Construction for the various environmental features incorporated in the construction of : a detached dwelling unit in Maple Bay. ¢ Elmworth Construction Ltd. for an attractive fourplex residential building that integrates well with the surrounding neighbourhood. 2005 - @ > The Crofton Seawalk, a volunteer project that opened up Crofton’s waterfront for everyone to enjoy. # Chemainus Post Office for its design that reinforces the overall small town heritage theme. ¢ Cascadia Forest Products for its project which resulted in an 80 per cent reduction in the saw mill's water 1 consumption. Council firmly believes in the importance of recognizing the efforts of those who develop projects that add to the Municipality, and we encourage you to consider nominating projects that you fee! are worthy. With your support, the Design Awards Program can become an enduring way to build pride in our community. Nomination forms for the 2008 Awards program are enclosed for your convenience. Nominations can be submitted to the North Cowichan Planning Department by August 15, 2008. Forms can be picked up at the Municipal office and on the website www.northcowichan.bc.ca. Nominations should include a brief outline of the project, its history, merits, and positive community impacts, as well as photos (digital, if possible). This will undoubtedly have an impact on i our business operations as well as on the feasibility for residents of the Gulf Islands to execute, much less afford to carry out home building and maintenance. Not only will commercial activity and prosperity suffer, but the environmental impact alone will be much greater. Instead of a ten minute drive for our trucks to reach the loading area, we will now have to truck our materials another 35 minutes away and embark on a significantly longer barge route. The fuel consumption and thusly, carbon output will be increased 's this not a primary concern of the Town of Ladysmith and have we not decided as a community to work against this kind of detrimental action? Furthermore, it is our understanding that the Town itself does not hold the lease to this land, but that it is indeed held by the Marine Society. if such is the case, how can this denial of access be permitted? There was a very similar case that came up in Nanaimo and the end result was in the favour of the residents and anyone wishing tc. access. the boat ramp. There were not any tolls imposed nor was access restricted. | We feel this decision should be re-evaluated and the concerns of the local businesses and residents be taken into account.
