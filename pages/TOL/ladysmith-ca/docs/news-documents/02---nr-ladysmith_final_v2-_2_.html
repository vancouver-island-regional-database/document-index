Ladysmith Receives Federal Funding for Sewage Treatment Plant Upgrade New Facility will be a First in North America January 19, 2012 Ladysmith, British Columbia – A state-of-the art secondary sewage treatment system, the first of its kind in North America, is being built in the Town of Ladysmith, thanks to over $5.2 million from Canada’s Gas Tax Fund. The upgrades to the aging structure will benefit Ladysmith’s residents as well as its harbour. “Our Government is proud to deliver permanent annual infrastructure funding for municipalities through Canada's Gas Tax Fund,” said James Lunney, MP for Nanaimo- Alberni, on behalf of the Honourable Denis Lebel, Minister of Transport, Infrastructure and Communities. “This investment will help protect our marine environment – an important part of our community and the local economy.” The innovative design of the new plant – a first in North America – is based on a model used widely in Europe, which features an exceptionally small environmental footprint compared to conventional technology. It is especially well suited for coastal towns or communities with constricted plant sites. The total cost of the project is estimated to be $16.2 million. “Our population has grown beyond the level that the existing treatment plant was designed to serve, and primary treatment of our waste is no longer acceptable,” said Rob Hutchins, Mayor of Ladysmith. “The new plant will contribute to improved water quality in our harbour, better quality of life for our community, and the continued growth and diversification of our local economy.” “This is a great example of innovative use of funding, towards effective wastewater management for a growing community,” said Parksville-Qualicum MLA Ron Cantelon. “Partnering through programs such as the Gas Tax Fund means that communities can make infrastructure upgrades possible sooner than they would be able to on their own.” Canada’s Gas Tax Fund provides stable, long-term funding to local governments and other organizations to help them build and revitalize public infrastructure. A program of Infrastructure Canada, the fund primarily supports capital projects such as local roads, public transit, energy systems and waste management infrastructure that lead to cleaner air, cleaner water or reduced greenhouse gas emissions. The Union of British Columbia Municipalities (UBCM) administers the Gas Tax Fund in BC in collaboration with Canada and British Columbia. On December 15, 2011, the Government of Canada passed legislation to make the Gas Tax Fund a permanent annual investment of $2 billion. “There is a general recognition that a higher standard of wastewater treatment is good for the environment and good for communities, but it comes at a cost,” said Heath Slee, President of UBCM. “I am very appreciative of the support that the Government of Canada is providing through the Gas Tax Fund for the core services that communities rely on.” For additional information about investments in infrastructure, visit www.infrastructure.gc.ca .
