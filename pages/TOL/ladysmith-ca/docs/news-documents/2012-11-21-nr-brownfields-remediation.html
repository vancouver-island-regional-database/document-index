REPORT ON REMEDIATION COSTS FOR LADYSMITH WATERFRONT RELEASED LADYSMITH, November 27, 2012 – The Province of B.C. and the Council of the Town of Ladysmith have released a long anticipated report on the environmental condition of the Ladysmith waterfront. The report outlines the extent of environmental contamination and gives a range of costs for alternatives to address remediation. Decades of industrial activity in the Ladysmith harbour, including; a copper smelter, coal washing, log sorting, and railway maintenance and repair have left a legacy of contamination. Golder Associates Ltd. was hired by the provincial government to carry out site investigations, sediment testing and geotechnical investigations on the Provincial Water Lots and Town lands. The testing identified the type and likely amount of contamination in various locations along the Ladysmith waterfront between Fisherman’s Wharf in the north and Transfer Beach in the south. With that information in hand, the consultants were able to provide a range of cost estimates for remediating the contamination. Costs for remediation would be largely determined by the type of development, and are currently estimated at $26.8 million for the development options considered for the upland, filled foreshore and marine areas. These costs do not include the costs for addressing the geotechnical stabilization of Slack Point or erosion control along the coastline. “The environmental remediation of the waterfront area is priority for the Town of Ladysmith and the Stz’uminus First Nation,” said Mayor Rob Hutchins. “We appreciate the financial commitment of the Province to undertake this NEWS RELEASE For Immediate Release comprehensive analysis and we look forward to further discussion with the Province on the development of a remediation plan. The good news within the report is that much of the Town’s uplands are free from contamination. One intriguing option is that the coal sediment (Slack Point and adjacent water lots) may have commercial value. We have begun exploring whether it can be extracted and sold, and put the proceeds towards remediation.” A copy of the Report on Remedial Costs for Ladysmith Harbour, and the preliminary investigation reports, are available on the Town’s website at http://www.ladysmith.ca/city-hall/reports-publications and on disc at City Hall. - 30 - For further information: Rob Hutchins Ruth Malli Mayor City Manager 250-245-6403 250.245.6401
