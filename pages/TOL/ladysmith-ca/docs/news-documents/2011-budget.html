COUNCIL APPROVES BUDGET WITH MODEST TAX INCREASE LADYSMITH, May 6, 2011 – Council has brought in a 2011 budget that focuses on maintaining services to residents while inve sting in long-term infrastructure, quality of life and citizen safety. The overall tax increase is 4.26 per cent, reflecting a raise of 4.96 per cent to residential property taxes, and 3.18 per cent to commercial property taxes, as well as a water parcel tax increase of $30 and a sewer parcel tax increase of $60. “Council continues to face the dual challenge of lower revenues from sources like developments and interest, and the increasing costs of providing the services Ladysmith citizens expect,” said Mayor Hutchins. “The modest tax increase in the 2011 budget will maintain services, and will also allow us to invest in water and sewer services for the long term, replace essential fire protection equipment, and enhance some public amenities. As in previous years, Council is continuing to shift away from the traditional reliance on heavy industry for most tax revenues. Since 1995, Council has continuously reduced its reliance on revenues from heavy industrial taxpayers. In 2011, heavy industry will contribute 14.89 per cent of the Town’s tax revenue, compared to 15.73 per cent in 2010. Over half of a total residential tax bill is for funds to pay for services such as school taxes, Cowichan Valley Regional District, Cowichan District Hospital, RCMP, and Vancouver Island Regional Library. Ladysmith’s property tax notices should be mailed out by the end of May. …/2 NEWS RELEASE LADYSMITH Highlights of the 2011 budget include: • Funding essential projects such as the water pipeline in the south end which allows the whole town to be fed from the covered Arbutus reservoir. This project also includes a hydro generation project to generate additional revenues for the Town (subject to grant) • Completing the Liquid Waste Management Plan and proceeding with upgrades to the waste water treatment plant • Extension of the low flush toilet rebate program, assisting and encouraging residents to reduce water consumption by offering $75 toward the cost of a new low-flush toilet • Repairs to the Stocking Lake Dam and the water line crossing Holland Creek, ensuring a continued safe water supply to the Town • Funding for replacement of essential fire protection equipment such as vehicles and hydraulic rescue tools • Investment in an upgrade to the Aggie Hall washroom • Stabilization of the Amphitheatre Bank at Transfer Beach • For the first time this year, the taxpayer is funding the trolley service through taxation. -30- Media Contacts: Rob Hutchins, Mayor 250-245-6403 rhutchins@ladysmith.ca Ruth Malli, City Manager 250-245-6401 rmalli@ladysmith.ca
