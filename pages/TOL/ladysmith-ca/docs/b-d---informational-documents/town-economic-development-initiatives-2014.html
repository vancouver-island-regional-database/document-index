36 Ways the Town helps to support Economic Development There are over 500 licensed businesses contributing to our local economy. Our businesses cover the spectrum from retail to construction, from services to manufacturing. Council has seized every opportunity we can to support our local businesses and to help them be successful. Measures we have taken include: 1. Promote local tax incentives such as the Revitalization Tax Exemption program for downtown business owners who carry out property improvements and the Economic Revitalization Tax Exemption Program for the creation of new businesses and jobs. 2. Created DL2016 Holding Corp which paved the way for funding and an operating agreement and servicing to Ladysmith Maritime Society to support the Community Marina visitor moorage and Welcome Centre. 3. Investing in significant water and sewer infrastructure improvements to ensure services exist for business and provide other core services like the Fire Department and RCMP. 4. Created the Rocky Creek light industrial / business park with reduced Development Cost Charges for investors. 5. Work with property owners and Stz’uminus First Nation to develop the South Ladysmith Industrial Park. 6. Collaborate with other agencies to bring programs to Ladysmith such as the ‘Climate Smart for Business’ program completed by the “49th Parallel Grocery Store”. 7. Host a “Business and Development Port al” on the Town’s website to provide a simple point of entry to local business information and resources (www.ladysmith.ca/business-development/overview) 8. Support the “10% Shift” Shop Loc al Initiative in partnership with the Chamber and the LDBA and promote the program on the Town’s website. 9. Installed new parking lot signage to highlight free public parking lots in the Downtown. 10. Offer the “BizPal” service through the Town’s website which offers a quick assessment or local, provincial and federal permit requirements for business. 11. Participate in the Partnership for an Economically Diverse Community with the Ladysmith Chamber of Commerce, the Ladysmith Downtown Business Association and the Stz’uminus First Nation. Council sponsors two meetings per year. 12. Introduced business-friendly checklists for business licensing and business signage. 13. Initiated business licence program options such as the Inter -Municipal Business Licence (Cowichan Region) and Inter-Community Business Licence (Malahat north) for mobile businesses. 14. Support the Chamber of Commerce to operate the Visitor Centre and start-up a Business Centre through funding an annual service contract which sees all Business Licence revenue going to the Chamber (unique in the Cowichan Region). 15. Publish an annual Community Profile of economic and community information providing local information for businesses looking to expand or investment in Ladysmith. 16. Involved the business community in the de velopment of the new Zoning Bylaw through Chamber and LDBA participation on the Stakeholder Committee resulting in changes such as: o Including a broader range of permitted uses within the Downtown and Coronation Mall and the two Business/Industrial Parks. o Creation of parking options and exemptions for businesses in the Downtown resulting in new business investment in heritage buildings. o Supporting home based business 17. Facilitated regional transit service locally to provide a way for our less mobile citizens to support local small businesses from one end of Ladysmith to the other. 18. Promoted Ladysmith as an investment ready community through the business -led “BC Small Business Roundtable” . I n 2011 , the Town was presented the award for the Most Small Business Friendly Community in the Vancouver Island/Coast Region. We were proud to make a submission for this award with letters of support from the Chamber of Commerce and the LDBA. 19. Created a tax policy that responded to business request to hold the line on commerc ial property tax increases, while also reducing our overall reliance on industrial taxes. 20. Developed a made-in-Ladysmith Economic Development Strategy with the support of the Ladysmith Economic Development Commission. 21. Partner with and contribute financially to the services delivered through the Cowichan Valley Regional District Economic Development Cowichan Department – economic development, regional tourism and film office. 22. Provided business licence information to Economic Development Cowichan for the regi onal BRE Cowichan First program. 23. Work with Economic Development Cowichan on a regional community profile and sub -regional Ladysmith profile in alignment with the Town’s Community Profile. 24. Provide Ladysmith collateral to the Economic Development Cowichan for trade-shows. 25. Welcome investors locally and internationally and provide Ladysmith’s community profile, economic sectors, and plain language business investment information. 26. Created an Industrial Land Inventory to assist investor inquiries. 27. Work with the Chamber and the LDBA in the update and refresh of the Town’s signage program to invite visitors off the TransCanada Highway and into Downtown Ladysmith. 28. Work with Ladysmith Secondary art class for student art banners in the Downtown. 29. Created new theme banners (shop, play, savour, indulge, explore) for the Downtown. 30. Publish heritage tourism brochures to encourage visitors to downtown Ladysmith and to stay longer. 31. Promote the heritage downtown in tourism advertising and on the tourism website. 32. Host the Touri sm Ladysmith website (www.tourismladysmith.ca) with a new mobile -friendly platform. 33. Participate in regional tourism promotion – Vancouver Island Travel Planning Map; Vancouver Island Travel Planner; Cowichan Travel Planner; Cowichan Free Map (joint with the Chamber). 34. Participate in local tourism promotion – Take 5 Ladysmith Visitor Guide; Chronicle Ladysmith Visitor Guide; Chronicle Chemainus Guide. 35. Maintain a professional photography inventory for local touri sm promotion including external photographic requests. 36. Built Forrest Field as a community asset to support sports tourism and participate in the regional sports tourism network.
